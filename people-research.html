People and Needs

Working conclusion

The strongest first audience to investigate is the nearby combination of residents, workshop and shop users, and people seeking practical learning. Business and creative users are a secondary audience to test through particular activities. Visitors from across the river could come for a specific event; they should not be treated as an automatic daily audience.

This is a priority for investigation, not confirmed demand. A recorded home or workshop establishes a relevant activity near the site, but does not establish dissatisfaction, willingness to attend, ability to pay, or a journey to the proposed building. These distinctions affect which spaces the project should prioritise and how their entrances and hours should work.

The defensible working proposition is a shared learning, work and exchange destination: a place where nearby people can meet, develop and present work, take part in small activities and pause comfortably. It must offer a useful addition to existing provision. Neither highway visibility nor shelter alone is a sufficient reason to visit.

Reading upper, lower and across

The map is north-up. Upper means north; lower means south; left means west across the Beirut River. These are positions on the drawing, not social classes or ground elevations. The plot is north of the main highway. Its closest mapped highway footbridge links the southern approach context with the northern side.

Four pale, dashed study windows organise the reading. Green covers a southern mixed neighbourhood context; ochre covers immediate working frontages; purple covers the northern business/production context; blue marks the western cross-river context. These are authored analytical windows, not surveyed land-use districts or statistical catchments. Their size and opacity convey no number of people. An individual can live in one area, work in another and visit from a third.

The map intentionally shows northern housing/mixed uses as well as southern workshops. The municipal account describes a district where shops, small workshops, restaurants and homes coexist, including workshop activity on streets branching from commercial routes. This supports a mixed reading, not a clean industrial/residential division. No undated municipal population or enterprise totals are used. Municipality: work and activities

Users, origin and reason to come

Southern residents: repeated local use

S1 represents residents of the lower-side mixed blocks, not a pin on a particular household. Possible reasons to visit include informal learning, small meetings, shared activities and a comfortable pause. These are hypotheses to ask about. Children, older people and carers are possible users, but their presence or numbers cannot be inferred from footprint size or storeys.

A place that requires crossing the highway must provide enough value to justify that effort. An attractive interior may still be irrelevant if the needed activity is already closer to home. Ask what residents use now, how often, what is inconvenient, and whether they would make this specific trip. Do not add childcare, a school or specialised clinical provision on the strength of a general statement about community need.

Design test: locate welcome, seating and public support where a first-time visitor can reach them without navigating a controlled office floor. Test whether learning rooms can operate after work without opening the entire building. This is a proposed relationship within the brief, not an established operating model.

Makers, shopkeepers and prospective learners

S2 and N1 separate local productive/trade users from a generic “community.” Owners, employees, customers and trainees have different time constraints and reasons to visit. A person working in a workshop does not necessarily need another workshop; they might need product presentation, a small client meeting, a specific tool or a skill unavailable at their own premises—or none of these.

UN-Habitat's 2017 Nabaa strategy recommended assessing local capacities and market opportunities, supporting business skills, and exploring apprenticeships between artisans and young people. This is a useful direction for questions, not proof of present demand at the northern plot. Nabaa is a different subarea, so its percentages and demographic characteristics are not applied to the shaded windows. Nabaa profile, livelihoods recommendations, printed p.32

Design test: connect a flexible learning/demonstration room with documentation, small meetings and a modest display area. This could let a piece of work move from making to explaining to showing. It is a proposal to validate with potential users. Equipment-heavy, dusty or hazardous processes would require a separate technical assessment; “workshop” does not justify importing industrial production into every shared room.

Same-side workers and existing amenities

Immediate northern frontage users may reach the plot without crossing this highway. Their short trips should not be represented as the same journey as a southern resident's approach. Yet a same-side location does not prove a continuous pavement, safe crossing of side streets or a usable entrance.

Record 207's café/restaurant is important counter-evidence to an easy but weak argument that “workers have nowhere to eat.” Current operation, price, seating and hours are VERIFY. The research question is whether a new offer adds something useful. Short-stay support, a bookable room or a comfortable non-purchase pause may be more relevant than duplicating food provision.

Northern business and creative activity

Sugar Rush describes media-production work and lists a ground-floor address at BCP Business & Creative Park on Seaside Road. This establishes a listed activity and destination context, not its staff's need for coworking, an available partnership or a route to the project. N2 is an approximate context pin; it is not a verified entrance. Sugar Rush, About/address

Potential offers include occasional project meetings, practical product-presentation sessions, mentoring or public showcases. Existing businesses may already provide these internally. They should be asked what they cannot currently access and whether local makers or learners would benefit from a shared activity on terms useful to both parties.

Design test: allow quiet, controlled work without making the whole project exclusive. A bookable room that can also support community teaching at other times is a possibility; room capacity, fees, booking and acoustic separation remain TBD.

Western visitors and the wider district

Forum de Beyrouth lists Charles Helou Avenue, Medawar as its address. Its presence across the river supports studying a separate direction of arrival. It does not establish that event visitors walk past this plot, need another venue or would extend a trip here. Forum contact

Treat these visitors as conditional: a named exhibition, meeting or workshop could justify a planned trip. Do not size a daily programme around assumed event spillover or regional traffic. The river crossing and the highway crossing are different obstacles and may occur in different journeys. The blue approach component shows the Armenia road bridge only; the onward walk is not asserted.

Further north and the waterfront

Q1 deliberately stops at a question. The shoreline lies outside the detailed saved base. The official brief identifies the industrial/logistical waterfront as context, but that does not locate particular workers, identify their exits or establish their freedom and time to leave a workplace. These people cannot yet be counted as a primary audience.

The next evidence would need to identify a real destination, its accessible exit, a viable trip and a reason to visit. There is no justified direct arrow from an unlocated port worker to the project and no claim that all northern users come from the waterfront.

Existing provision changes the question

The district is not an institutional blank. The municipal study's historical education discussion identifies training and cultural provision while arguing for further technical formation. That combination matters more than old numerical deficits: existing services and additional needs can coexist. Present capacity, costs and accessibility must be checked anew. Municipal study, printed pp.18–19

Mouvement Social lists a Bourj Hammoud centre on Al Masaken Street and describes its training, artisanal-production and community role. Its page names a wider regional reach. It is a possible organisation to consult, not an agreed partner or a centre placed at S3. Mouvement Social

YFC describes a vocational programme begun in 2019, including computer and sewing training and English classes. This is provider-reported provision from a faith-based organisation; it does not measure district-wide unmet need or imply universal access. Its current enrolment capacity and eligibility were not established. YFC vocational programme

Ifpo's November 2025 Aswat programme documents a mapping workshop centred on Royal Cinema and its surroundings. That is evidence of a historical cultural-learning activity, not an ongoing schedule or a verified current facility gap. Ifpo Aswat programme

Consequence: ask what these providers cannot offer, when their spaces are available and which audiences do not reach them. Then ask potential learners independently. The new project might provide complementary rooms or sessions; it should not claim to create learning or culture where none existed. No organisations were contacted and no partnerships are agreed.

Arrival determines who can benefit

The existing Connections study identifies Street 52, a mapped highway footbridge with associated stairs, and Street 80 on the plot side. These are candidate approach components, not a verified continuous walk. OSM bridge 701135687, Street 52, Street 80

C1 asks who may struggle with that approach. Stairs affect more than wheelchair users: carrying materials, pushing a stroller or needing frequent rests can change the practicality of a visit. The existence of stairs neither proves a usable step-free alternative nor proves there is none. It remains VERIFY. A lift inside the project cannot establish accessible arrival from another side of the highway.

No walking times, density circles or shortest-path catchments are shown. Those would require a validated pedestrian network including vertical changes, gates and landing connections. Dashed coloured segments follow preserved source components; gaps remain gaps.

Staff and operators are essential users even though their home origins are unknown and therefore unmapped. A greenhouse, exhibition room or workshop without a maintenance and staffing plan is a programme label rather than a demonstrated service. Their operational requirements belong in the design brief without implying that recruitment or operating finance has been solved.

A day to test

The map offers a qualitative schedule hypothesis: short workday visits and meetings; learning and shared activity after school or work; occasional evening/weekend events. This is not observed footfall. Work patterns vary and some users may be unavailable at all these times.

Test opening hours and duration with each group. A short-break user may need a direct route to seating; a class may need reliable booked access and storage; an evening event needs an entrance that operates when offices are closed. Separate these operating conditions before assuming that one room can serve all uses simultaneously.

Testing Nesting against people

Nesting is useful only if protection enables the intended activities. The public arrival can lead to a sheltered shared space, with quieter work or teaching protected farther inside or above. But increasing enclosure can also make the building difficult to read, exclusive in appearance, or inconvenient to enter.

Three design tests follow: can a passer-by understand what is offered without entering an office lobby; can a local session operate when business floors close; and can the quiet learning/work areas be protected from the workshop and service route? These questions can guide plans and sections without claiming that the site analysis has determined a final massing.
