ENTERING BEIRUT · CONCEPT STUDY / 02

From layers beside each other to layers belonging together.

Layers is the concept. Nesting is the act: one setting holds another, while the spaces and openings between them keep them connected.

1. The city gives us the conditions

The bridge already connects the two sides. Our question is what happens as people move through these different conditions—and arrive beside our plot.

2. Nesting changes their relationship

We do not fold the actual city into a building or copy seven bands as seven rooms. We borrow the idea of distinct layers, then test a different relationship between them.

The important part is the space between the layers. It becomes a shared place, not a leftover gap or only a corridor.

3. On our plot, the layers receive an actual arrival

The main pedestrian arrival comes from the south after the C1 stairs. A covered threshold can receive it; shared space can hold smaller settings beyond. The east walk-in joins the same welcome, while vehicles reach underground parking separately.

On a narrow screen, pan this drawing sideways. Ribbons show relationships, not walls or a selected building shape. Open ground remains outside the compact test envelope.

The jury story

I take the layers from the city, but change their relationship on our plot. Instead of only passing from one condition to another, people enter a shared setting that holds smaller ones. That is nesting: each layer keeps its character, while the spaces and openings between them bring them together.

Protection supports this arrangement; it is not the reason for calling it nesting. The project works on the quality of the local arrival, not a claim to repair the whole city.
