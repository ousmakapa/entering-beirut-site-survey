Urban Connections

Finding

The plot belongs to several overlapping networks rather than three isolated districts. Regional roads, local streets, crossings and destinations perform different jobs. The useful design question is which existing local journey the project could strengthen, and where that journey changes from through-movement into an approachable building entrance.

The interactive map separates those networks. Its shaded areas are interpretative study windows, not measured land-use boundaries. It does not claim that every northern building is industrial or every southern building residential. Existing survey facts, photographs and building assignments are unchanged.

Geographic scope

The detailed drawing uses the preserved 2026-09-13 OpenStreetMap extract and the current Pictures New base, approximately 1.2 km across. The project boundary is the locally aligned survey polygon, not a newly surveyed boundary. The actual shoreline is beyond this base. The river, coastal-side road and sea must not be treated as interchangeable destinations.

There are two separate directions of connection: across the river toward Beirut/Karantina, and across the highway between southern streets and northern business areas. A crossing in one direction does not solve the other. Dora's larger transport interchange is a district-context reference, not the footbridge beside the project.

Regional movement and local continuity

The highway's source geometry includes carriageways and ramps with bridge/layer tags. These distinctions matter: lines crossing in a plan are not necessarily intersecting at ground level. The red network communicates regional road hierarchy, not measured traffic volume, congestion, noise or pedestrian accessibility. Road thickness is a drawing convention.

The municipality describes commercial/craft streets as an internal linking network, identifies mixed activities in the highway–Armenia strip, and describes Dora's regional transport role. This supports reading connections through activities as well as roads. It is a historical baseline, not a current count of traders, commuters or operating bus services. Municipal study, p.8

Analytical consequence: a regional connection and an easy neighbourhood walk are separate qualities. The map identifies their meeting points without assuming either total disconnection or frictionless access.

The plot-side chain

The preserved OSM geometry identifies Street 52 south of the highway and Street 80 beside the plot to the north. Street 80 meets Seaside Road. Between them, way 701135687 is tagged as a bridge and footway. Separate stair geometries are ways 270786366 and 701135688. The bridge's two-point plan centreline measures approximately 57.8 m, computed from geographic coordinates. This excludes stair travel, approach distance and any vertical rise. OSM bridge, Street 52, Street 80

The map's Plot approach view isolates the sequence:

  1. Southern local streets provide the approach context.
  2. C1 marks the mapped highway footbridge and its stairs.
  3. The northern landing must connect to the local street edge.
  4. Street 80 provides the plot-side approach and continues toward Seaside Road.
  5. BCP is a listed destination in the northern business context.

This is a candidate pedestrian chain assembled from mapped components, not a verified walking route. No diagonal line is inserted to pretend that missing sidewalk/landing details are known. The current open/closed condition, stair access, landing clearance, curb transitions and exact plot entrance are VERIFY / TBD. A staircase cannot establish step-free access, but its presence also does not prove that no alternative exists.

Design consequence to test: resolve the landing–street–entrance sequence before inventing an additional district connector. An entrance visible from a bridge may still require an awkward approach; visibility alone does not complete that sequence. This is not a proposal to attach the building to the existing bridge.

Crossing choices

The extract contains another bridge-tagged footway east of C1, 1069243892, with associated approach/stair ways. C2 marks it separately. C3 instead marks the Armenia Street road bridge across the river, ways 452148075/452148076. Eastern footbridge, Armenia bridge

These are selected mapped crossings within a limited extract, not an exhaustive inventory of usable crossings. The distinction matters more than an unsupported access score: pedestrians may have to choose a crossing according to where they are going, not simply the nearest point on the highway. No preference, crossing delay or route usage is measured here.

Beirut Urban Lab's 2021 Karantina discussions distinguish the provision of overpasses from how safe residents felt using them. This provides a relevant question for fieldwork, not a safety diagnosis of C1 or C2. Strategic Diagnosis, connectivity discussion

Destinations and relationships

Sugar Rush's own page lists BCP Business & Creative Park on Seaside Road. The map uses the earlier project-reference coordinate for the complex, explicitly marked approximate; it does not identify an entrance. The listing supports a creative-production destination, not a proven commute from the southern neighbourhood. Sugar Rush, About/address

DEMCO lists its headquarters in Dora Seaside/Bourj Hammoud. The local survey separately documents auto-service and other commercial frontages. The D2 tag describes this context; it is deliberately not a precise DEMCO entrance pin. Proximity does not establish supply chains, employment patterns or collaboration between firms. DEMCO, Contact

Forum de Beyrouth lists Charles Helou Avenue, Medawar as its address. Its named OSM footprint on the western bank gives a separate destination for cross-river study. Event schedules, entrance location and event-related pedestrian movement are TBD. Forum, Contact

There is evidence that everyday relationships already cross neighbourhood boundaries: Urban Lab's 2021 fieldwork records Karantina residents using clinics in Bourj Hammoud. It does not establish their precise route, mode or any passage by this plot. No origin–destination flow arrow is drawn for an unlocated clinic. Strategic Diagnosis, p.77

Analytical consequence: distinguish a known destination, a documented inter-area relationship, and a specifically traced route. None automatically proves the other two. A programme can respond to nearby activities, but the case for shared services needs more than a collection of business pins.

The shoreline question

Seaside Road serves coastal-side destinations; its name is not evidence of a continuous route to the water. The northern study window stops at the detailed-base limit. Q1 marks an unresolved onward connection, not a shoreline location.

Urban Lab's estuary and open-space network material describes proposed connections. Those proposals are not drawn as existing paths here. No claim is made that the waterfront is completely inaccessible either. Karantina Action Plans, pp.48–49

What this analysis supports

The project should first be evaluated as a reachable destination beside an existing crossing. Its possible contribution is local: making arrival from that crossing and the surrounding streets legible, continuous and compatible with the activities inside. A larger coastal role remains a question until an onward route and relevant destinations are established.

This conclusion does not yet determine massing or prove Nesting. A sheltered interior could still fail if its entrance is detached from the approach network. Conversely, an effective local entrance does not require claiming to reconnect all of Bourj Hammoud to the sea.
